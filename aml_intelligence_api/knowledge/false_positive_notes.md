# False Positive Case Notes

These examples show situations where an alert was generated but the activity was reasonably explained.

## Case Note 1: High-value salary payment

An alert triggered because a customer received £12,000 in one payment.

The customer was a senior consultant with expected monthly activity of £10,000 to £15,000. The payment came from the customer's known employer and matched previous salary activity.

Outcome: recommendation for closure.

Reason: the amount was consistent with the customer's occupation, expected activity and known source of funds.

## Case Note 2: International family transfer

A customer sent £4,000 overseas, triggering an unusual corridor alert.

The customer regularly sent similar amounts to a close family member. Previous transactions showed the same beneficiary and the source of funds was verified salary income.

Outcome: recommendation for closure.

Reason: the activity was consistent with established account behaviour and supported by customer information.

## Case Note 3: Missing explanation

A customer made several transfers that appeared unusual, but no transaction purpose was available.

Outcome: request information.

Reason: insufficient evidence existed either to escalate or recommend closure.

## Guidance

Analysts must not treat every alert as suspicious. A false positive may be recommended for closure where:
- activity is consistent with the customer profile;
- the source of funds is reasonable;
- counterparties are known and explainable;
- historical behaviour supports the activity;
- no additional suspicious indicators are present.
