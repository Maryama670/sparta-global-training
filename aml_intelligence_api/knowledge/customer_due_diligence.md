# Customer Due Diligence Procedure

Customer due diligence is used to determine whether observed activity is consistent with the customer's known profile.

## Required checks

1. The analyst must review the customer's occupation or business activity.
2. The analyst must review expected monthly account activity.
3. The analyst must review the declared source of funds.
4. The analyst must compare actual transaction values and frequency against expected activity.
5. Material differences between expected and observed activity must be investigated.
6. The analyst must identify whether the transaction purpose is known.
7. Where information is missing, the analyst must recommend requesting further information.
8. The analyst must not assume that high-value activity is suspicious solely because of the amount.
9. The analyst must consider whether the activity has a reasonable explanation based on the customer's known circumstances.

## Warning signs

Examples include:
- activity substantially above expected monthly levels;
- unexplained international transfers;
- transactions inconsistent with occupation;
- sudden changes in transaction frequency;
- source of funds that does not explain the observed activity.

## Example

A student has expected monthly activity of £1,500 and receives family support as the stated source of funds. The account then sends £8,000 overseas.

The analyst must compare the transaction against the customer profile. The amount is materially above expected activity, so additional information about the source and purpose of the funds should be requested before recommending closure.
