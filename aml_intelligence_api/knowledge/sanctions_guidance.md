# Sanctions Screening and Escalation Guidance

This procedure explains how analysts must handle alerts involving a possible sanctions match.

## Required steps

1. The analyst must review the customer, counterparty and transaction details associated with the alert.
2. The analyst must compare the name, country, date of birth, business name and other available identifiers against the sanctions screening result.
3. A partial name match must not be treated as a confirmed sanctions match without further evidence.
4. Where identifying information is missing, the analyst must request additional information before recommending closure.
5. If the available identifiers strongly match a sanctioned person or entity, the analyst must recommend escalation.
6. The analyst must record which identifiers matched and which did not match.
7. The analyst must not tell the customer that a sanctions investigation is taking place.
8. The analyst must not automatically close an alert solely because one identifier differs.
9. Where the evidence is insufficient to confirm or reject the match, the analyst must recommend further investigation.

## Warning signs

Warning signs may include:
- exact name and date of birth matches;
- matching nationality or country information;
- repeated transfers involving a screened counterparty;
- payments involving an entity whose details closely resemble a sanctioned organisation;
- attempts to change beneficiary information after a screening alert.

## Example

A customer sends £7,500 to a company called Eastern Shipping Holdings. The screening system identifies a similarly named sanctioned entity. The company name is a close match, but the registered address is missing.

The analyst must not automatically conclude that the counterparty is sanctioned. The analyst must compare all available identifiers and request the missing company registration and address information. If several strong identifiers match, the analyst should recommend escalation.
