# Mule Account Typology Guide

A mule account is an account used to receive, move or withdraw funds on behalf of another person or criminal network.

## Warning signs

Potential indicators include:

1. Sudden increases in incoming and outgoing transfers.
2. Funds received and rapidly transferred elsewhere.
3. Multiple unrelated counterparties sending money to one account.
4. Activity inconsistent with the customer's age, occupation or expected monthly activity.
5. Repeated transfers with little money retained in the account.
6. Several linked accounts using the same counterparties.
7. Customers unable to explain the purpose or source of incoming funds.

No single warning sign proves that an account is a mule account. Analysts must assess the indicators together.

## Analyst procedure

1. Review incoming and outgoing transactions.
2. Identify repeated or shared counterparties.
3. Compare transaction activity with the customer profile.
4. Review linked alerts.
5. Identify whether funds move rapidly after receipt.
6. Request information where transaction purpose or source of funds is unclear.
7. Recommend escalation where multiple indicators remain unexplained.

## Example

A student account normally receives £1,200 per month. Over three days it receives transfers from six unrelated individuals totalling £14,000, then sends most of the money to two overseas counterparties.

The analyst should investigate the relationship between the customer, senders and beneficiaries. The activity is inconsistent with the expected account profile and contains multiple mule-account warning signs.
