# Suspicious Activity Reporting Procedure

This procedure describes when an AML investigation should be considered for suspicious activity reporting.

## When to consider reporting

An analyst should recommend escalation for suspicious activity reporting where the available evidence indicates activity that cannot reasonably be explained and may involve money laundering, fraud or other financial crime.

## Required steps

1. Complete the investigation before recommending a report.
2. Record the relevant alerts, accounts and transactions.
3. Describe the suspicious activity clearly and factually.
4. Explain why the activity is inconsistent with the customer's known profile where relevant.
5. Reference linked counterparties and related alerts.
6. Avoid unsupported assumptions.
7. Record what information remains unknown.
8. Escalate the case to the authorised reporting team.

The analyst must not submit an external report solely because an automated system recommends escalation.

## Narrative quality

The narrative should explain:
- who is involved;
- what activity occurred;
- when the activity occurred, if timestamps are available;
- where the funds moved;
- why the activity is considered suspicious.

## Example

Three unrelated customer accounts make similar-value transfers to the same beneficiary. The customers provide inconsistent explanations and cannot evidence the source of funds. The analyst documents the transactions, linked accounts and missing explanations and recommends escalation for consideration of suspicious activity reporting.
